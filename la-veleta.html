La Veleta, Tulum: the honest guide

What it is, what it costs, where to eat, and what nobody tells you before you stay or buy here.

What La Veleta is

The inland residential neighbourhood northwest of Tulum centre. It is not the beach and does not pretend to be. Jungle with streets, low buildings, ongoing construction and a growing number of cafes.

This is where the people who stayed actually live: Mexicans from other cities, long-stay foreigners, people who work online. That is why the housing here is whole houses rather than hotel rooms.

Some developments call it Uptown. Same place. On the map and in the land registry it is still La Veleta, Region 11.

Sargassum, without the spin.

April to October, worst June to August.

Anyone who guarantees you a clean beach is selling you something.

Stay inland, use the cenotes, and it stops being your problem.

What nobody tells you

If you are staying for weeks or thinking about buying, this matters more than the photos.

Water, power, internet

Tap water is not drinkable across most of the area, except where a building has its own reverse osmosis and UV. Ask before you book. Outages happen: the serious developments have a generator and solar. Fibre reaches much of La Veleta, but confirm the real speed if you work online.

Streets and construction

Paved streets and dirt streets, sometimes on the same block. Construction is still active: if you are coming to rest, ask what is on the other side of the wall. In the rainy season the unpaved streets get difficult.

Getting around

A bike for daily life, a moto if you go to the beach often, a car if you have kids or want the further cenotes. Taxis have no meter, so agree the price before you get in.

Tax if you rent it out

Quintana Roo charges lodging tax, Tulum charges a per room per night sanitation fee, and there is the state Visitax. Registration on the state tourism registry is mandatory. Talk to an accountant in Quintana Roo before you sign.
